### 5.3 ⭐ The compressor→reap rule is now a threshold plus a population

The 11-11 report left this at five data points and a threshold stated as "~5.2–6.3 GiB, not a
round 5". Today supplies the missing test and answers it **four times over**.

The kernel logged **1 200 `idle-exit` lines = 600 unique PIDs**, in **four bursts**:

| Burst | Window | PIDs | Compressor at the time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15:18:56 → 15:39 | **300** | **423 725 pages = 6.46 GiB** |
| 2 | 20:11 → 20:13 | 100 | 369 9xx pages = **5.64 GiB** |
| 3 | 20:41 → 20:43 | 100 | 369 9xx pages = **5.64 GiB** |
| 4 | 21:11 → 21:13 | 100 | 369 8xx pages = **5.64 GiB** |

Two things fall out of that table:

1. **The threshold is real and it is not 5 GiB.** 5.04 GiB produced **0** reaps on 11-11;
   **5.64 GiB produced 300 across three bursts** today. The line sits between those, and
   "~5.2–6.3" can be tightened to **"between 5.04 and 5.64 GiB"**.
2. **Burst size is not a function of how far over the line you go.** 5.18 GiB once fired **535**;
   6.46 GiB fired **300**; 5.64 GiB fired **100 three times in an hour**. A threshold alone
   cannot explain that ordering. What does explain it is **the population of idle daemons waiting
   at that moment** — 100 is the size of a fresh cohort, 300 is three cohorts, 535 is a
   long-idle machine with a full backlog. **Reaps = threshold crossed × daemons available.**

A third observation, and it is a correction to the instrument: **the sampler never saw the peak.**
Its highest compressor reading all day was **5.63 GiB at 21:15**; the kernel's own line caught
**6.46 GiB at 15:18**, between two 15-minute samples. The 1 200 reap lines carry a
`compressor_size:` field that is the only witness to the day's true peak. **Read the kernel's
compressor field, not just the sampler, when hunting this threshold.**
