## 2. Disk — the audit's experiment finally ran, four days late

### 2.1 The result

The 10-29 audit found **20.6 GB** of per-update `*.code_sign_clone` directories in the per-boot
temp tree and could not prove causation, because per-day steps did not map 1:1 to clone counts. It
therefore declared **the reboot the test**: expect **268–272 GB** afterwards, and if the reading did
not fall by **≥ 15 GB**, the clones were not the whole story.

| Check | Prediction (10-29) | Observed (11-02) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disk used after reboot | **268–272 GB** | **276 GB** | **4–8 GB high** |
| Drop across the restart | **≥ 15 GB** | **−16 GB** (292 → 276) | ✅ **passes its own threshold** |
| Clone tree | should collapse | **20.6 GB → 1.6 GB** | ✅ collapsed |
| Temp tree total | — | **2.4 GB** (was > 20 GB) | ✅ |

**Both halves have to be stated together, because they disagree.** The drop is real and larger than
the threshold, at a moment when *nothing else* about the machine's storage changed (same volume,
same 1 OS-update APFS snapshot, still **0** Time Machine snapshots, SMART **Verified**). But 276 GB
is **not** inside the predicted 268–272 GB band, and the arithmetic says why: the clones measured
**20.6 GB**, and the disk gave back only **16 GB** — while two ordinary days at the established
**~1.5 GB/day** drift would have *added* ~3 GB first. Gross reclaim is therefore ≈ **19 GB**
against **20.6 GB** of clones, i.e. **92 %**. The remaining 8 % is not a contradiction, it is the
rest of the machine growing while the clones were deleted.

**Verdict: the clone leak is confirmed as the dominant driver of 10-18 → 10-30, with a ~1.6 GB
residual.** It is not confirmed as "the only thing that grew" — and the 10-30 report's honest
"today's +2 GB is unattributed" remains unattributed, because one reboot cannot date it.

### 3.2 The counters, normalised per 24 h — and this is the interesting part

Cumulative counters reset at boot, so they cannot be differenced against 10-30. They can be

| `Decompressions` | 719 911 pg = 11.0 GB | **1.41 M pg = 21.5 GB** | 10.71 M pg = 164 GB | **0.13×** |
| `Pageins` | 2 382 461 pg = 36.4 GB | **4.66 M pg = 71.1 GB** | 4.395 M pg = 67 GB | **1.06×** |
| `Pageouts` | 15 985 pg = 0.24 GB | 31 274 pg = 0.5 GB | — | — |
| `Swapins` / `Swapouts` | **0 / 0** | **0 / 0** | +28 039 / +102 084 | **∞ lower** |

**Compression fell 4.1×, decompression 7.7×, and page-ins did not move at all (+6 %).** That
decomposition is the finding: if the 214 GB/day had been *file I/O or demand paging*, page-ins
would have collapsed with it. They did not. What vanished is **compression churn specifically**, and
the compressor's occupancy fell **2.4 GB** with it.

Two readings are possible and this report will not pretend to choose:

1. **Uptime-dependent** — the compressor's working set slowly fragments over days, so churn scales
   with time-on, and a reboot is the cure. This fits 10-28 (0.35 M/day, day 9) → 10-29 (9.1 M) →
   10-30 (14.0 M), a ramp *within one boot*, and it fits today's drop.
2. **Work-dependent** — the 10-29/10-30 ramp was a genuinely heavy two days and this is a light one.

Against (2): today's page-ins are *identical* to 10-30's, which is the best available proxy for
"the same amount of real work". That tilts the evidence to **(1)**, but one sample after a reboot
cannot distinguish "uptime" from "the two days before the reboot". **Watch condition in §7.**

## 5. Anomalies — 0 panics, 0 kills, and one restart nobody can explain

### 5.1 The instrument came back

The 10-30 report recorded a **capability regression**: `/usr/bin/log` returned *"Cannot run while
sandboxed"* (exit 64), `ps` returned nothing, so the kernel-record census was impossible and a
carried-forward question ("name the compression driver") was declared unanswerable.

**That has reverted.** `log show` exits **0**, `ps -Ao pid,comm` returns a normal process table, and
the census below was produced from them. So the 10-30 limitation was **an environment/sandbox
condition of that run, not a property of the machine** — the wording in that report ("this
instrument is gone") was too strong, and this report corrects it. Treat the instrument as
**per-run**, and re-test it every run rather than citing a remembered state.

sample was taken, and the intervals were 8–44 h apart. **Absence in a 12 h morning window is weak
evidence** — it needs a full evening cycle to mean anything.

### 5.3 The restart

| Evidence | Value |
|---|---|
| Boot time | **2026-11-01 19:39:23** (`kern.boottime`) |
| Last event of the previous boot | **19:32:08** — a wake from idle sleep at 83 % |
| Next event | **19:39:40** — `powerd process is started` (the new boot) |
| Clean-shutdown sequence (`loginwindow` / `shutdownd` / sync) | **none found** |
| `kern.shutdownreason` | **`wdog,reset_in_1 ap_restart`** |
| Corroborating panic file | **none** — but the diagnostics store is missing (§5.4) |
| Uptime destroyed | **14 d 2 h** (boot of 10-17 21:15) |

A clean restart leaves a shutdown sequence in both the power log and the unified log. Here the
power log simply **stops** at a normal wake and resumes with a fresh `powerd`. Combined with a
shutdown reason that names the **watchdog**, the most probable reading is an **abrupt reset at some
point in 19:32–19:39**, not a menu-initiated restart.

**This is stated as a probability, not a conclusion**, for three reasons: the diagnostics store that
would hold a panic log does not exist; `ap_restart` in that string is not decoded by anything
available here; and a **user-initiated restart would look almost identical** if the shutdown
sequence fell outside log retention. If the restart was intentional, this finding closes and should
be deleted from the trend. If it was not, it is the **first unexplained reset in 27 reports**, and
the 14-day uptime it ended is exactly the uptime the clone leak needed, so a coincidence is
plausible — a hang under that much temp-tree I/O is not excluded either.

### 5.4 Diagnostics store: absent, so file-based zeros mean nothing

- `PANIC_FILES=0` and `JETSAM_24H=0` in today's row are **absence of evidence**, not evidence of

- The kernel-log census in §5.2 is what carries today's "0 panics" claim, and it is the stronger

## 8. Method

`bash scripts/collect.sh` for the published row (sysctl / vm_stat / pmset / df / diskutil only, no
sudo, no identifiers), plus, for this report only: `sysctl kern.boottime kern.shutdownreason`,
`pmset -g log` (rotated store, 7 597 lines, covering 10-31 23:41:58 → 11-02 07:55),
`pmset -g custom`, `ioreg -c AppleSmartBattery`, `diskutil apfs listSnapshots`,
`tmutil listlocalsnapshots`, `du -xh -d2 /private/var/folders`, `find` on the clone tree, and
`log show` with predicates for panic / memorystatus / kill families.

Per-boot temp-tree paths and clone directory names embed **application names** and are therefore
reported as **"a browser's per-update code-sign clone"**; diagnostic file names embed the host name
and are counted, never printed. All per-24-h figures are **uptime-normalised**, not extrapolated
from a single instantaneous sample.

**The perturbation, measured.** A second `collect.sh` pass at **08:07**, run after the `log show`
sweeps above, read **9.47 GB / 59.2 %** RAM and load **2.05 / 2.86 / 2.56** — against **10.24 GB /
64.0 %** and **2.12 / 1.81 / 1.95** at 07:55. That is the collector measuring its own investigation
(the 5-min average rose 58 %). **The row in `data/history.csv` is the 07:55 pre-work sample**, and
the 08:07 reading is recorded here only so the discrepancy is not mistaken for a later correction.
Same rule as 10-28 and 10-29: report the pre-work sample, label the post-work one.
