## 8. Repository changes, the CSV row, and what this run corrected

### 8.1 🟡 The README gains two distinctions, both from numbers that double

Nothing in the previous documentation was wrong today. Two things it did not say are now written
down:

- **Count kills by `killing_idle_process`, not by `killing`.** The kernel writes two lines per
  reap, about a second apart — the same doubling the README already documents for
  `charge_sessions`, in a different subsystem. Grepping `killing` on today's log gives **2 233**
  lines for **1 117** kills, i.e. a 92/h rate instead of 46/h (§5.3). A short section with the two
  literal lines as an example is added, plus a note that a `per-process-limit` kill also produces
  a `JetsamEvent-*.ips` and must not be counted twice.
- **The swap pool grows back, and there is a third direction of change.** The existing section
  said the pool "shrinks without a reboot"; it now records 2 048 → 3 072 → 2 048 → 3 072 across
  six days with no restart in between, and names **eviction** as the third way `swap_used_mb` can
  move — `swapouts` up, compressor down — alongside paging and discarding (§5.2).

### 8.2 🟢 The CSV row

### 8.3 🟡 The out-of-repo sampler and the snapshot are four hours apart, and it shows

The 15:45:04 sample read **10.47 GB / 65.4 % / 3.82 GB compressor / 2 095 MB swap**. The snapshot
at 19:49:07 reads **11.37 GB / 71.1 % / 5.48 GB / 2 063 MB**. A 15-minute series and a point
sample taken four hours later are not supposed to agree, and on three of the four fields they do
not: used memory rose ~0.9 GB and the compressor ~1.7 GB in that gap while the machine sat at 76 %
on battery. The one field they agree on is **swap, flat within 32 MB** — which is the honest
reading of the last four hours: memory pressure built, but nothing more was written to disk.

The sampler's schedule is the limitation, not its accuracy: it is driven by a scheduler that sleeps
with the machine, so it stops when the lid closes and resumes when it opens, and it reports
nothing for the final stretch of every interval.

### 8.4 Pitfalls re-confirmed

`/usr/bin/log` by absolute path; read the first line of `pmset -g log` before quoting a window;
`du -sh` on the Update volume is meaningless; `diskutil apfs listSnapshots` misses volume-group
snapshots; `DIAG_24H` can be sampled mid-burst and is a rolling window, not the interval; the
`.diag` `SFA` burst follows a **plug-in** at 10 s (§1.2); each `powerd` state post and each
`memorystatus` kill can be logged twice (§3.4, §5.3).
