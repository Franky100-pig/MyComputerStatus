Read honestly, that line is weaker evidence than it first looks. **Two of the three occurrences are

capacity records climbed 86 → 99.

What the log cannot say is **who** disabled it. The policy arrives as a message from a user-space
process — `received SOC limit from <pid>: <private>`, `SOC limit policy:<private>` — and the value is
redacted, so the difference between "the OS decided this is a calibration charge" and "the limit was
switched off by hand" is invisible here. The cadence is suggestive: **11-19 (100 %), 11-20 (90 %),
11-21 (100 %), then five days flat at 85, then today (99 %)** — a full charge every six days is what
periodic recalibration looks like, and it is what the README already asserts. But this log has been
wrong before when it promoted a plausible cadence to a mechanism (§8.4), so today's entry is
**observation: the limit can be lifted, and here is the log line** — not "macOS calibrates every six
days".

What is measurable is what the lift cost: the pack sat **above 90 % for 3 h 4 m** (10:26:32 →
13:29:54), of which **2 h 1 m awake** and 1 h 3 m asleep. That is a wear-relevant number this log has
never had before, and it is the one to watch if full charges become frequent.

*inside* the 13:06 burst), only in reverse: **the kernel allocates and releases the pool to fit the
working set, and the reaper is the visible half of that fit.**

The direction of the 1 600 MB fall is the interesting part, because the flow counters rule out the
two obvious readings:

| Counter (whole interval) | Δ pages | Δ bytes |
|---|---|---|
| `swapins` | 22 503 | **352 MB** |
| `swapouts` | 3 235 | **51 MB** |
| swap used | — | **−2 070 MB** |

So of the 2 070 MB that left swap, **352 MB was paged back in** and 51 MB written out. The remaining
**~1.67 GB was discarded** — pages that left swap because the VM objects owning them were destroyed,
not because they were needed. That is the third of the README's three directions, in its purest form:
yesterday's entry named *evicted* (swapouts up, compressor down), 11-24's was *discarded* (~1.4 GB),
and today's is the same mechanism with a pool shrink layered on top.

The correct sentence for the CSV is therefore **"swap used fell 2 757 → 687 MB, and only 17 % of that
was a recovery"** — the rest is memory that will never be read again because its owner exited.

### 6.3 🟡 Eighth staged run, and `du` was being read in the wrong unit

installed: OS build 25F71, boot epoch unchanged.

Yesterday's report wrote "**964 MiB by `df` … and 964 MB by `du -sx -m`, versus the 15 G that plain
`du -sh` would read**", and the day before wrote "`du -sx` **1 928 MiB**". Those two numbers are the
same measurement twice, and the second one is **wrong by a factor of two**:

```
$ du -sx /System/Volumes/Update
1974320    /System/Volumes/Update      ← 512-byte blocks, not 1 KiB blocks
$ du -sx -m /System/Volumes/Update
965        /System/Volumes/Update      ← 965 MB
```

**964 MiB**, and it agrees with `df` to the megabyte. The "df and du disagree" note that has been in
the README since 11-22 was an artefact of reading `du`'s raw block count as KiB. Corrected in §8.2.

What is real is the other half of the note: plain `du -sh /System/Volumes/Update` reads **15 G**,
because without `-x` it descends into the nested sealed-system and support mounts. That one is a real
trap and it stands.
