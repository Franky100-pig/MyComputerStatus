Three things follow, and only the first is comfortable.

**One: the jump is real and it is dated.** 1 725 → 4 416 MB between two samples 15 minutes
apart, with the **14:36:15 reap burst sitting in the gap**. This is the **2nd confirmed**
disk/swap ↔ reap coincidence — and the series is shorter than the 11-16 report claimed,
because its own instance was the misdated one corrected below.

**Two: the pool demonstrably resizes both ways, and it does so without leaving evidence.**
`used = 4 416` cannot coexist with a hard 3 072 pool. The pool must have been ≥ 4.4 GB at
14:42 and shrunk back to 3 files before I looked. `swapfile` creation does not appear in the
unified log at all (searched explicitly, one hit and it was this command's own argument), so
**file birth times are the only witness — and deleted files leave none.** This is why the
"check total, not used" advice from 11-16 forward-item 1 is not enough on its own: the total
is a moving target too.

**Three — a correction to yesterday's report.** The 11-16 entry dated `swapfile1` and
`swapfile2` to **11-16 18:17:58/59**. Their actual birth times are **11-15 18:17:58/59**. The
"two swapfiles created in the same second as a reap burst" observation from yesterday is
therefore **misdated by exactly 24 hours**, and the coincidence it was used to support is
weaker than it read. Today's 14:36 instance is independent and still stands.

**The drain is the other half of the story.** 4 416 → 2 116 MB is **2.3 GB released over
21 hours**, roughly **110 MB/h**, in even steps, while RAM stayed around 10 GB and even rose
to 13.00 GB at 20:20. Pages leaving swap without arriving in RAM means they are being
**discarded**, not paged back — file-backed pages whose cache was dropped. So a falling swap
number is not "pressure easing"; it is "cache being thrown away". This is the same trap as
yesterday's "the RAM mean fell because 5 GB moved to disk", in the opposite direction.

count would not catch it. **Adding a `memorystatus`-based fatal counter to `collect.sh` is the
correct fix**, and it is cheaper than waiting for a directory that has been gone for six days.
