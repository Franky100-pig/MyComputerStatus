does not.

### 3.5 🟡 `charge_rate_max = 0.0` — a blind spot, not a stall

The CSV row says `charge_rate_max = 0.0`, and the pack demonstrably gained 5 points. Both are
true.

`collect_charging.py` scores a ramp only **inside an AC segment that was created by a power-source
transition**, and measures from the level at the moment of plug-in to the peak before unplug. With
`ac_plugs = 0` there is no such segment, so no ramp is scored and the default `0.0` is emitted.

That is a defect in the metric on this particular shape of day, and it is worth writing down
because the number is not obviously missing — `0.0` reads like "the battery did not charge",
which is the opposite of what happened. The measured truth, from the capacity curve:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Average over the refill (80 → 85, 43 m 53 s) | **6.8 pts/h** |
| Steady state (81 → 85, 17 m 00 s) | **14.1 pts/h** |
| First percent (80 → 81) | **27 m** for one point = **2.2 pts/h** |
| Yesterday's full charge, for scale | **21.7 pts/h** |

The 27-minute first percent is what drags the average down to 6.8 — the other four points all took
about four minutes. Splitting the average into "the first point" and "the rest" turns a
mysteriously slow charge into an ordinary one with a slow start, and the slow start is what you
expect when the charger has just been told to stop holding and start charging: the current ramps
from zero rather than stepping to its limit.

This is the same class of error as the 11-21 sleep-gap artefact, and it needs the same rule: **do
not quote a single rate for a multi-point segment.** Quote the steady-state rate and say what the
first point cost.

Two events, two mechanisms, both already seen once:

- **The drop at 20:44 is a discard.** 433 MB left the pool with `swapouts` frozen at 870 805. The

- **The burst at 11:14 is real eviction.** `swapouts` rose by **44 192 pages = 691 MB**, which
  accounts for 682 of the 682.5 MB the pool grew by. This is the second burst in two days
  (11-21: +716 MB with `swapouts +47 328`), so "a large allocation pushes anonymous pages to
  disk" is now a **repeated observation**, not a one-off.

Yesterday's burst was 9 minutes after a download finished, which suggested a download-driven
mechanism. Today's has no download in front of it, and what *is* in front of it is different:

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 11:10:03 → 11:11:43 | idle-reap episode, 100 kills (§5.3) |
| **11:14:27** | **swap +682 MB, `swapouts +44 192`** |
| ≈11:18 | diagnostics store re-created (§1.3) |
| 11:58:29 | charger re-engages, 80 → 85 % (§3.3) |

The reaping episode ends **2 m 44 s** before the burst, and the compressor was already at 6.13 GB
by then (6.44 GB at 10:29, 5.71 GB at 10:59). Used memory also jumped at the same moment,
11.26 GB → **12.25 GB**. So the burst is not "the compressor being drained" — compressor and
resident memory rose together. It is a workload allocating, with the kernel spilling the cold
half to disk.

The refined statement: **the burst is eviction under genuine pressure, it recurs, and it is
coincident with the reaping episodes rather than caused by them** — both are symptoms of the same
allocation. The download hypothesis from 11-21 is not disproved; it is simply not necessary.

### 6.2 ⭐ `du -sh /System/Volumes/Update` says 15 G. It is wrong by a factor of 15.

This is the one genuinely new trap of the run, and the README recommended the exact command that
falls into it:

```
du -sh  /System/Volumes/Update   →  15G      # follows into nested mounts
du -sxh /System/Volumes/Update   →  967M     # stays on the one filesystem
df -h   /System/Volumes/Update   →  967Mi    # authoritative
```

Two other filesystems are mounted **inside** that directory and `du` counts them as if they were
contents:

| Mount | Device | Size used |
|---|---|---|
| `/System/Volumes/Update` | `disk3s4` | **967 MiB** |
| `/System/Volumes/Update/mnt1` | `disk3s1` (sealed) | **16 GiB** |
| `/System/Volumes/Update/SFR/mnt1` | `disk2s1` | **2.1 GiB** |
| — `softwareupdate.97184.*` staging dir, on `disk3s4` | — | 365 MB |

`966 M + 16 G + 2.1 G ≈ 15 G`, which is where the number comes from. None of the 16 GiB belongs
to the update — it is the mount point of the sealed system volume — and none of it is recoverable
by `softwareupdate`.

The practical rule: **`df` on the volume, `du -sx` if you want to see what is inside it.** The
15 GB figure did appear legitimately in the 11-20 report as a `df` reading, so this is not a
correction of that finding — it is a warning that the same number can now be produced on a day
when nothing is there.
