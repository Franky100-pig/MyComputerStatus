### The pool shrank without a reboot — and this log said it couldn't

`/System/Volumes/VM/swapfile*` birth times date every expansion, and today they also date a
**contraction**:

**Reading it:** the pool grew in two +1 GB steps yesterday (11:12 and 15:28) and gave one back
today at **20:26:24** — **28 h 58 m** after `swapfile3` was created, and **26 m 44 s after the
charger was plugged back in** (20:19:40). `ls` now shows **three** files, `vm.swapusage` reports
**3 072 MB**, which is 3 × 1 024.

**The correction, stated plainly.** Yesterday's diagnosis wrote that the pool *"never shrinks
without a reboot — it ratchets 1 024 → 2 048 → 3 072 → 4 096"*. That was an inference from a
handful of days and it was **wrong**. The kernel **does** release an emptied swapfile. What is true
is narrower and more useful:

> The pool grows in **discrete +1 GB steps** and is released in **discrete −1 GB steps**, only when
> a whole file drains. It therefore **looks** monotonic over short windows with nonzero usage —
> the ratchet was an artefact of always sampling while pages were still resident.

This matters for the verdict rule: **"pool growth" is a weaker signal than this log has been
treating it as** (it fired as a trigger on 10-18, 10-20 and 10-25). Today shows the pool can undo
itself, so growth alone should never carry a 🔴 without the traffic number beside it.
