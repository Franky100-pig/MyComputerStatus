### 3.3 ⚠️ A 10-point jump in one second — a gauge artefact, not a fast charger

At 20:38:45 the log prints, in the same second:

```
20:38:45.052  State:Charging On Hold  VBUS:1  UISOC:63   TLC:Fri Nov 13 20:22:33
20:38:45.204  State:Charging          VBUS:1  UISOC:72   TLC:Fri Nov 13 20:38:45
20:39:54.977  BDC  Battery percentage last 63 now 73
```

So charging was **held for 16 minutes at 63 %** (20:22:33 → 20:38:45) and then resumed with the
pack reporting **72 %**, one second later reporting **73 %**. Nine to ten points arrived in
under a second; no cell absorbs 10 % of a 4 600 mAh pack in one second.

Two readings follow, and this log should not pick between them on one observation:

- **Smoothing reset.** The published `Battery percentage` is a filtered value; the hold released
  the filter and the true number appeared. Then the true rate was continuous and the *displayed*
  63 % was the wrong number.
- **Gauge re-estimate.** The pack re-read open-circuit voltage during the hold and revised its
  estimate upward. Then the true rate had a step in it.

Either way the practical rule is the one this log already applies to `DailyMinSoc`: **do not
derive a charge rate from a segment that contains a `Charging On Hold` transition.** Here is what
that does to the arithmetic:

| Segment | Points | Duration | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 49 → 63 (before the step) | 14 | 1 h 07 m | **12.5 pts/h** |
| 72 → 85 (after the step) | 13 | 0 h 35 m | **22.3 pts/h** |
| whole session, as printed | 36 | 1 h 42 m | **21.1 pts/h** |
| whole session, step excluded | 26 | 1 h 42 m | 15.3 pts/h |

The three figures disagree by 1.8×, and the printed one happens to agree with the 19–21 pts/h
band from every previous clean session. **Quote the session total (49 → 85 in 1 h 42 m) and say
the curve has a step in it**; do not quote a rate.


clamshell blocks is consistent with a lid that was closed and a user who walked away; the two
Idle blocks are the display turning off with the lid open. This is a configuration fact, not a
fault, and it explains why a plugged-in day has *fewer* long sleeps and *more* short ones — the
opposite of intuition.

| Accounting | Asleep | Awake |
|---|---|---|

matters for the conclusion those reports drew, because it makes awake time a *larger* share and
dark wakes a *smaller* cost — reinforcing, not weakening, the "dark wakes are free" finding.

One burst, 100 PIDs, and the three-day record streak (535 → 600 → 700) **stops dead**. Every
victim is the same idle-helper cohort, idle times 32 000–34 000 s, each kill 3 MB, with **6.5 GB
available** — housekeeping, not pressure.

But the more important thing today is a **logic error in the 11-13 report**, and it should be
corrected rather than propagated:

> The 11-13 report wrote: *"The threshold is between 5.64 and 6.15 GiB. 5.64 GiB fired three
> bursts on 11-12; 5.04 GiB fired nothing on 11-11; 6.15 GiB fired 100 today."*

**A firing level cannot bound a threshold from below.** If the kernel reaped at 5.64 GiB, then the
threshold is **at or below 5.64**, not above it. A later firing at 6.15 — or today's at 6.33 — is
consistent with any threshold ≤ 5.64 and adds no information. The valid bracket was, and still is:

| Observation | Constrains | |
|---|---|---|
| no burst at 5.04 GiB (11-11) | threshold **> 5.04** | lower bound, valid |
| burst at 5.64 GiB (11-12) | threshold **≤ 5.64** | upper bound, valid |
| bursts at 6.15, 6.20, 6.33 GiB | nothing new | upper bounds only |

> **Correct statement: the compressor threshold is somewhere in (5.04, 5.64] GiB and today's
> evidence cannot narrow it.** The level recorded at a burst is an **overshoot**, and it varies
> from 5.64 to 6.60 GiB — 0.96 GiB of spread — because the check is periodic, not level-triggered.
> The variable that actually differs between days is **how much memory pressure accumulates
> between two checks**, which is why 700 PIDs fired on the busy day and 100 on the quiet one.

Two supporting facts from today. The machine was **asleep for most of the 24 h**, so the compressor
had little chance to build; and the single burst fired at the **highest level yet observed**,
6.33 GiB, with the **smallest cohort**, 100. If the trigger were a level, a higher level should
produce a *bigger* burst. It produced the minimum. **Burst size tracks the idle population, not
the overshoot** — which the 11-12 report already said, and which now has a clean test.
